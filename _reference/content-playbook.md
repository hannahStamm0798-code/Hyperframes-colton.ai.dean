# Content Playbook: @laurelksanti

How every idea is shaped before it's built. Read alongside `CREATOR.md` and `PREFERENCES.md` before planning any Reel, carousel or Story.
Sources: the "ChatGPT prompts for gynecologists" carousel by Daria Medric (studied 2026-11-16), trend research (2026-11-11), and what has performed for Laurel.

## The core rule
**Stop explaining what the topic is. Start explaining why someone should care.**
People don't wake up wanting to learn about hormones; they wake up wondering what's happening to their bodies. Open on a moment the viewer recognises, never a topic name.

| Boring topic | Problem-first hook |
|---|---|
| Progesterone | "You're 44 and suddenly wide awake at 3am. Here's what might be going on." |
| Blood sugar | "Crashing every afternoon at 3pm? It may not be your coffee." |
| Gut health | "You eat well and you're still bloated. Why are you being told it's just stress?" |
| Protein | "You're eating less than ever and feel weaker. That's not a coincidence." |

Hook sources: client frustrations, unexpected questions, misconceptions, Laurel's professional opinion. Conversational, emotionally relevant, no jargon.

## Formats (rotate)
- **Checklist:** "Signs your ___ may be ___" — Laurel's best performer (estrogen signs).
- **"3 reasons" / patient problem:** "3 reasons melatonin helps you sleep but you still wake up exhausted."
- **Myth breaker:** "It's just your age." / "Your cortisol belly might not be cortisol."
- **Unpopular opinion:** "You don't need to cut out entire food groups to balance your hormones."
- **Expert POV:** "Why I don't give every client the same plan." (ties to the Hormone Assessment)
- **Client story:** anonymous — no name, age or diagnoses; check permission first.

## Every post has a job
| Content type | Purpose | Examples made |
|---|---|---|
| Patient problems | Attention / reach | 3pm crash, melatonin, eating less after 40 |
| Myth vs. fact | Shares | cortisol belly, "just your age" Story, 5 hormone myths carousel |
| Checklists | Saves | estrogen signs, progesterone signs, protein signs, 3am carousel |
| Talking Reels | Familiarity / trust | **gap — none yet; ~60 talking clips available** |
| Clinical POV | Authority | "same plan" Reel, carousels |
| Assessment content | Enquiries | client-story Story, booking Stories |

Reels reach non-followers best; carousels earn the most saves; saves and DM shares are weighted heavily. A balanced week covers 3+ purposes and spreads booking asks out.

## 30-second talking Reel structure
1. **0–3s Hook** — the problem in the viewer's words
2. **3–8s Tension** — a relatable frustration
3. **8–23s Expertise** — one evidence-based insight in everyday language
4. **23–30s Takeaway** — one thing to remember or do
No "Hi, I'm Laurel and today…" intro.

## One topic → ten pieces
Pick 2–3 topics a month and spread each across a checklist Reel, a myth Reel, a talking Reel, a carousel, a Story poll, a client story, an expert-POV caption.

## Trend context (Nov 2026)
- Hormone content is crowded and ~4 in 10 women's-health posts are ads → **trust is the differentiator.**
- Backlash against "cortisol belly" and supplement-heavy content → Laurel's evidence-based "here's what's real" angle stands out.
- Hot topics: creatine for women (modest evidence), protein, perimenopause going mainstream.
- Lo-fi, real footage beats over-produced Reels.

## Captions
- Lead with a relatable line, deliver what the post promised, end with ONE ask (question, share, save, or booking).
- 3–5 focused hashtags, e.g. #hormonehealth #perimenopause #hormonebalance #womenshealth + one topic tag.

## Guardrails
- No fear-mongering or exaggerated claims; keep uncertainty where it's real.
- No individualised advice in public content — that's what the assessment is for.
- Never identifiable client details.
- AI packages Laurel's expertise; it never invents it. **Laurel checks every health claim.**
- CTAs point to Laurel, never "see your doctor".

## Don't copy from references
- Daria Medric's black-serif/red-italic visuals — Laurel's navy + blush stays.
- Profanity / edgy tone — Laurel is warm and clinical.
